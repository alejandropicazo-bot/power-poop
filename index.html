POOP POWER! 💩⚡

Can animal poop really make electricity? Explore the science, become an energy detective, and create a PSA that surprises your audience.

Explore • Investigate • Create
🐄
Start with a prediction

What if yesterday's waste could power tomorrow's lights?

Cows produce manure every day. Usually we think of it as something smelly to clean up. But with the right technology, it can help make renewable energy.

Big idea: Poop does not turn directly into electricity! Tiny living organisms break down manure and create a gas. That gas can fuel equipment that generates electricity.

Make a prediction: Which part do you think does the most important work?

Meet the vocabulary

Four words to know

Manure: Animal poop used or collected on farms.

Microorganisms: Tiny living things, including bacteria, that are too small to see without a microscope.

Biogas: A gas mixture formed when microorganisms break down organic material without oxygen.

Digester: A special sealed tank where this breakdown happens.

Science reality check

Does every pile of poop automatically power a lightbulb? No! It takes a carefully operated digester, gas collection equipment, and a generator. This is real technology—not a do-it-yourself classroom experiment.

Safety: Do not handle animal manure or try to collect or ignite biogas. Your challenge is to explain the science with drawings, narration, paper models, or stop-motion animation.
